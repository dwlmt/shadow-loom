This policy governs **content created with, ingested into, or generated by**
Shadow Loom — whether you run it yourself under [AGPLv3](LICENSE), under a
[commercial license](COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md), or via a hosted instance

It is intentionally **permissive**. Shadow Loom is a tool for writers,
screenwriters, game and IF authors, narrative designers, editors and
researchers. Serious fiction routinely engages violence, crime, sexuality,
politics, war, abuse, addiction, and moral darkness — half the bundled
example worlds (Macbeth, Apocalypse Now, Reservoir Dogs, Gone Girl, 1984,
Frankenstein) would be unusable under a sanitised policy. We do not
intend to gate legitimate creative work.

  derivative work of *that prose*, not of Shadow Loom. Your right to
  publish it depends on your right to use the input — see §3.

## 2. Permitted creative content

Shadow Loom is intended to handle the full range of adult literary
content. The following are **explicitly permitted** as narrative material:

* Graphic violence, on-page death, war, torture, crime, weapons.
* Morally complex, villainous, or unsympathetic protagonists.
* Adult sexuality between fictional adults, including explicit content.
* Drug use, addiction, self-harm, suicide as story elements.
* Profanity, slurs in character voice, hate speech depicted (not endorsed)
  by fictional actors.
* Politically, religiously, or ideologically charged themes.
* Real public figures in clearly fictional, satirical, historical, or
  alternate-history contexts.
* Horror, body horror, psychological abuse, dark romance, grimdark.
* Existing IP and fan fiction (subject to §3 — *your* responsibility, not
  the tool's).

The auditor and physics modules will not refuse content on moral grounds.
They check causal, epistemic, and continuity consistency — not taste.

## 4. Hard prohibitions

These are the only categories Shadow Loom asks you not to produce. They
are narrow, and they exist because they cause real-world harm regardless
of fictional framing or because they expose every operator (you, us,
the LLM provider) to criminal liability:

1. **Sexual content involving minors**, in any framing, style, age-up,
   or "they're actually 1000 years old" fictional device. No exceptions.
2. **Operational uplift for mass-casualty harm** — working synthesis,
   acquisition, or deployment instructions for chemical, biological,
   radiological, nuclear, or high-yield explosive weapons, dressed as
   fiction or otherwise. Depicting that such weapons exist in your story
   is fine; providing a working recipe is not.
3. **Functional malware, exploitation tooling, or instructions for
   attacking specific real systems**, presented as fiction or otherwise.
4. **Non-consensual intimate / sexual imagery or text depicting a
   real, identifiable living person** ("deepfake" sexual content).
5. **Targeted harassment, doxxing, or fabricated criminal accusations
   against a real, identifiable living person.**
6. **Direct, credible incitement to violence against a real, identifiable
   person or group** — distinct from depicting violence in a fictional
   world, which is permitted.
7. **Any use that is criminal in your jurisdiction.**

If you are writing a thriller in which a character *plans* a bioweapon
attack, that is fiction and falls under §2. If the prose contains a
working procedure that would meaningfully help a real attacker, that is
§4(2) and is prohibited. The line is *operational uplift*, not
*subject matter*.

## 6. Hosted Service operated by the copyright holder

Where Shadow Loom is offered as a hosted service operated by the
copyright holder ("the Hosted Service"), the following additional terms
apply. They will be re-stated in the Hosted Service Terms of Use; this
section is a plain-language summary.

### 6.1 No training on your content
Your ingested prose, world models, generated outputs, audit reports,
and graph edits **are not used to train any model** operated or
commissioned by the copyright holder.

### 6.2 LLM provider passthrough
The Hosted Service routes generation, extraction, and audit prompts to
a third-party LLM provider (OpenRouter and the upstream models it
fronts). Your inputs are transmitted to that provider and are subject
to **that provider's retention, logging, and usage policy** — which the
operator of the Hosted Service does not control. A current list of
providers is published in the Hosted Service Terms.

### 6.3 Retention and deletion
Your world models, versions, and account data are retained until you
delete them. On account or project deletion, data is purged from
primary storage promptly and from backups within the backup-rotation
window stated in the Hosted Service Terms.

### 6.4 Per-account isolation
The Hosted Service is **single-tenant per account**. Every project,
world model, version, ingested prose, generated scene, audit report,
graph edit, and **external research lookup** is scoped to the account
that created it. There is no shared library, no public feed, no
cross-account discovery, and no user-to-user content exposure inside
the product. You can only access content that you (or an agent acting
on your behalf via the MCP server, see §6.7) have ingested or
generated under your own account. Cached results from the optional
research provider (see §6.9) are likewise keyed to your account and
never reused for another user. The bundled example worlds shipped
with the open-source distribution are the only content visible by
default and contain no material restricted under §4. Operator staff
may access account data only as strictly necessary for support,
security, abuse investigation, or to comply with legal process — see
the Privacy Notice for details.

### 6.4a Optional external research provider
Some Service operators may enable an optional **research provider**
(e.g. Tavily) that performs web searches when you explicitly request
one (via the `research_topic` MCP tool, or by running extraction with
`EXTRACTION_ENABLE_RESEARCH_AGENT=true`). When this feature is active:

- Provider calls are made **only on your explicit instruction**, never
  silently or as part of normal extraction.
- Snippets returned by the provider are **segregated background
  context only**. They populate `WorldStateV1.world_facts`, which is a
  separate field from entities, events, beliefs, and world traits.
  The pipeline is structurally prohibited from promoting a research
  snippet into the canonical world model.
- Generation may consult these snippets for *texture* (period detail,
  vocabulary) but the structured world state remains the sole source
  of truth about characters, plot, and world rules.
- Research results are cached per-account (§6.4) and may be
  inspected, edited, or deleted by you at any time via the
  `list_world_facts` / `delete_world_fact` MCP tools.
- You are responsible for the queries you send to the provider and
  for verifying any factual claim before relying on it. The provider
  is a third-party service operating under its own terms; the
  operator passes your query through and is not the source of the
  results.
